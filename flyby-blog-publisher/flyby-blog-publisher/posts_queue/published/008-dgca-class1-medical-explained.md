<p>Before you invest a single rupee in flight training, there's one requirement worth checking first: the <strong>DGCA Class 1 Medical Certificate</strong>. It's the fitness standard every commercial pilot must meet, and unlike ground exams or flying hours, it's not something you can simply study harder for. Here's what it actually involves.</p>

<h2>Vision Standards</h2>
<p>You'll need <strong>corrected visual acuity of 6/6 in each eye</strong>, along with normal colour perception, typically assessed using Ishihara plates. If you wear glasses or contact lenses, correctable vision generally meets this standard — but colour vision deficiency (colour blindness) can be a more serious obstacle, so it's worth getting tested early rather than assuming.</p>

<h2>Cardiovascular Requirements</h2>
<p>An <strong>ECG (electrocardiogram)</strong> is mandatory from your very first Class 1 examination, and the cardiovascular standards applied are notably stricter than the Class 2 medical (used for private pilots). Examiners assess heart rhythm, blood pressure, and overall cardiovascular fitness against thresholds appropriate for the physical demands of commercial flying.</p>

<h2>Managing Existing Conditions</h2>
<p>A pre-existing condition doesn't automatically disqualify you. Pilots managing conditions like hypertension or diabetes can often still be certified, provided they submit updated specialist reports demonstrating the condition is well-controlled and within acceptable limits. This is exactly why we recommend an early medical assessment — so any manageable condition is identified and addressed before you commit to training, not discovered midway through.</p>

<h2>Cost</h2>
<p>Initial Class 1 examinations typically range from <strong>₹8,000 to ₹15,000</strong> depending on the city and examination centre. Renewals are somewhat cheaper, usually <strong>₹5,000 to ₹10,000</strong>. Costs vary by centre, so it's worth confirming the current fee directly with your chosen examiner before booking.</p>

<h2>Validity Period</h2>
<p>Your Class 1 Medical Certificate isn't a one-time formality — it needs regular renewal:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Under 40 years old:</strong> valid for 12 months</li>
<li><strong>40 years and above:</strong> valid for 6 months</li>
</ul>
<p>Missing a renewal deadline invalidates your certificate and suspends your ability to exercise commercial flying privileges until it's renewed — so this is one date every working pilot tracks carefully throughout their career, not just during training.</p>
