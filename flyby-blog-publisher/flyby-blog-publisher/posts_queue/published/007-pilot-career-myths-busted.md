<p>Every year, we meet students who assume they're not "cut out" to be a pilot — often based on misconceptions rather than actual DGCA requirements. Here are five of the most common myths we hear, and the reality behind each one.</p>

<h2>Myth 1: "You need to have studied Science with top marks in school"</h2>
<p><strong>Reality:</strong> You need Class 12 with Physics and Mathematics — not necessarily as your only stream, and not necessarily with exceptional marks. If you came from Commerce or Arts, you can still qualify by completing Physics and Maths through NIOS. Aptitude for flying and discipline in ground studies matter far more than your Class 12 percentage.</p>

<h2>Myth 2: "Only the very wealthy can afford pilot training"</h2>
<p><strong>Reality:</strong> Pilot training is a genuine financial investment — realistically in the ₹15,00,000–₹25,00,000 range for a full CPL — but it's not exclusively for the ultra-wealthy. Many students fund training through education loans, staged payment plans, or family support saved specifically for this purpose. What matters more than being wealthy is planning your finances realistically from day one, rather than being surprised by costs partway through training.</p>

<h2>Myth 3: "There's a strict height or physical requirement to fly"</h2>
<p><strong>Reality:</strong> There's no rigid height cutoff for a CPL in the way many students assume. What actually matters is passing your DGCA Class 1 Medical Certificate, which assesses vision, hearing, cardiovascular fitness, and overall health — not arbitrary physical measurements. If you're unsure whether a specific condition might affect your medical, get an early assessment rather than ruling yourself out based on assumption.</p>

<h2>Myth 4: "Once you get your CPL, you're guaranteed an airline job"</h2>
<p><strong>Reality:</strong> A CPL is your entry ticket, not a guarantee. Airlines generally look for a Type Rating and often an MCC (Multi-Crew Coordination) course on top of your CPL, and hiring cycles depend on airline growth and industry demand at the time you're job-ready. Choosing a flying school with strong placement guidance — not just flight hours — makes a real difference here.</p>

<h2>Myth 5: "Cabin crew and pilot training are basically the same path"</h2>
<p><strong>Reality:</strong> These are genuinely different careers with different training, timelines, and requirements. Cabin crew training focuses on safety procedures, service, and passenger handling, and takes considerably less time than pilot training, which requires DGCA licensing, flight hours, and technical ground exams. Both are legitimate, rewarding aviation careers — but they're not interchangeable, and choosing the right one for you starts with understanding what each actually involves day to day.</p>
