<p>With a training investment in the range of ₹15–25 lakh and 18–24 months of your life, choosing a flying school is one of the most consequential decisions in your entire pilot career. Yet many students choose based on a glossy brochure or a single visit. Here's what's actually worth evaluating.</p>

<h2>1. DGCA Approval and Track Record</h2>
<p>Confirm the school (and any partner flying club it uses for actual flight hours) is DGCA-approved and has a track record of students successfully completing their CPL. Ask directly: what percentage of recent batches completed training within the expected timeline, and what's the average total hours-to-completion?</p>

<h2>2. Fleet Condition and Availability</h2>
<p>Aircraft availability directly affects your training timeline. A school with an aging or frequently-grounded fleet, or one that's overbooked relative to its aircraft count, can mean months of delays waiting for flying slots — which extends your timeline and, often, your total cost.</p>

<h2>3. Ground School Faculty Quality</h2>
<p>The five DGCA theory papers plus RTR(A) are where many students actually struggle, not the flying itself. Ask about faculty experience, pass rates on first attempts, and whether ground classes are structured and scheduled, or informal and self-paced.</p>

<h2>4. Transparent, Complete Cost Breakdown</h2>
<p>Be wary of a headline number that doesn't include everything. Ask specifically what's included and what's billed separately: ground classes, simulator time, actual flying hours, DGCA exam fees, study materials, and any repeat-hour charges for weather delays or failed check rides. A transparent school will walk you through this without hesitation.</p>

<h2>5. Location and Weather Patterns</h2>
<p>Flying hours can be significantly delayed by poor weather at certain times of year in certain regions. Ask how the school handles seasonal weather disruptions and whether they have contingency plans (alternate airfields, adjusted scheduling) to keep your training on track.</p>

<h2>6. Placement and Career Guidance</h2>
<p>A CPL alone doesn't guarantee an airline job. Ask what happens after licence issuance: does the school offer guidance on Type Rating selection, MCC courses, and airline application processes? Or does support end the moment you're licensed?</p>

<h2>7. Talk to Current and Former Students</h2>
<p>Marketing materials tell you what a school wants you to hear. Actual students and alumni tell you what training was really like — delays experienced, faculty responsiveness, and whether the total cost matched what they were originally quoted. Ask the school to connect you with a few, or look for independent reviews.</p>
