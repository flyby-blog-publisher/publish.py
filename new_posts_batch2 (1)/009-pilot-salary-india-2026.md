<p>One of the most common questions we hear from students partway through their training is simple: "What will I actually earn once I'm flying?" It's a fair question given the size of the investment in pilot training. Here's a realistic breakdown based on current industry figures.</p>

<h2>First Officer Salary</h2>
<p>Across Indian airlines, First Officers typically earn between <strong>₹2,00,000 and ₹3,50,000 per month</strong> — roughly ₹24–42 lakh annually. This range varies by airline type:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Budget carriers</strong> (like SpiceJet) tend toward the lower end, roughly ₹15–30 lakh annually</li>
<li><strong>Full-service carriers</strong> (like Air India), particularly for pilots certified on widebody aircraft, offer higher compensation within this range</li>
</ul>

<h2>Captain Salary</h2>
<p>Once you've upgraded to Captain — which requires significantly more flying hours and an ATPL — compensation rises substantially:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Domestic narrowbody Captains:</strong> ₹5,00,000–₹10,00,000 per month (₹60 lakh–₹1.2 crore annually)</li>
<li><strong>Widebody/senior Captains:</strong> ₹12,00,000–₹20,00,000+ per month</li>
</ul>
<p>Air India tends to lead on Captain compensation (₹80 lakh–₹1.5 crore+ annually), while IndiGo offers competitive pay (₹60 lakh–₹1.2 crore) alongside notably faster command upgrades — often within 5–7 years of joining as a First Officer.</p>

<h2>The Big Jump: First Officer to Captain</h2>
<p>Captains typically earn <strong>2.5 to 3 times</strong> what First Officers earn — arguably the most significant single salary jump in the entire career path. This upgrade usually becomes possible somewhere between your 6th and 10th year of flying, depending on the airline, your accumulated hours, and internal seniority/promotion cycles.</p>

<h2>What This Means for Your Career Planning</h2>
<p>The financial case for a pilot career isn't just about the entry-level salary — it's about the trajectory. A First Officer's early-career earnings already comfortably outpace most graduate salaries in India, and the Captain upgrade represents a genuine step-change a few years in. That said, actual figures vary by airline, aircraft type, seniority, and market conditions at the time, so treat these as planning estimates rather than guarantees.</p>
